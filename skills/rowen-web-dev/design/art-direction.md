# Art Direction

Before coding a new or substantially redesigned interface, define a concrete direction rooted in the product.

Identify:
- who uses it;
- what they are trying to accomplish;
- the subject matter/industry/material/vernacular;
- the emotional tone;
- the primary visual idea;
- the memorable detail;
- what common template convention this project should avoid.

Design from the subject matter rather than from a generic SaaS component library.

A bold direction does not require visual loudness. Minimalism can be distinctive through typography, spacing, composition, material, imagery and interaction.

Do not reject common fonts/components merely because they are common. Reject choices that are lazy, contextless or make the project interchangeable with unrelated products.

Name actions from the user's perspective and keep terminology consistent through the flow.

## Typography is part of art direction

Do not postpone font selection until after the layout is effectively finished. Typeface, measure, weight, tracking and hierarchy materially change composition and identity.

Consult `typography.md` before locking the visual direction.
